Complex-Valued Models Applied to Power Systems Analysis - Power Flow & Power System State Estimation -
Descrição: Nonlinear optimization problems in complex domain are frequently encountered in applied mathematics, e.g., signal processing, control theory, neural networks and biomedicine, to name a few. The solution of these problems often requires a rst- or second-order approximation of the objective function to generate a new step or descent direction to meet the solution iteratively. However, such methods cannot be applied to real functions of complex variables because they are necessarily nonanalytic in their argument, i.e., the Taylor series expansion in their argument alone does not exist. To overcome this problem, the objective function is usually redened as a function of the real and imaginary parts of its complex argument so that standard optimization methods can be applied. Although not widely known, it is possible to construct an expansion of the objective function in its original complex variables by noting that functions of complex variables can be analytic in their argument and its complex conjugate as a whole. This property lies in the fact that if a function is analytic in the space spanned by realfzg and imagfzg in R, it is also analytic in the space spanned by z and z* in C. The main contribution of this work is the application of this methodology to a complex Taylor series expansions aiming algorithms commonly used for solving nonlinear weighted least-squares problems applied to power systems. But before doing that, a complex-valued load ow (CV PF) modeling through Newton-Raphson method is revisited and enhanced. Nonetheless, especially emphasis is addressed to Gauss-Newton method when derived in complex domain for solving power system state estimation (CV PSSE) problems, whichever they are applied in transmission or distribution systems. The factorization method applied to the complex Jacobian matrix emerged from CV PF and CV PSSE approaches are the Three Angle Complex Rotation (TACR) algorithm that is derived from the Givens Rotations algorithm in real domain. In this research one demonstrates that Wirtinger derivatives can lead to greater insights in the structure of both problems, i.e., CV PF & CV PSSE. Moreover, it can often be exploited to mitigate computational overhead, storage cost and enhance the network's component modeling, besides easily handle PMU measurements and embedding new technologies towards smart grids. These results will be presented in forthcoming papers to be submitted to IEEE-PES Transactions on Power Systems and coming up Top Conferences..

NSF CPS: Frontiers: Collaborative: Addressing Trade-offs Between Robustness, Resiliency and Sustainability in Smart Grids.?
Descrição: Electric power systems are undergoing radical changes characterized by an evolution toward more flexible infrastructures, termed Smart Grids, and triggered by the deployment of new technologies in power electronics, sensing, protection, control, and communications. While these technologies are opening new avenues for enhanced system reliability, resiliency, and sustainability and for enabling proactive involvements of customers in the management of the system, they also introduce new vulnerabilities that, if not properly identified and mitigated, may offset their potential benefits. We propose to address these challenges in two areas, namely, the Science and Engineering of cyber-physical systems. Thus, we will use a systems engineering approach to achieve the following: (i) lay down a theory of resiliency for the massive cascading failure of complex systems; (ii) develop methodologies and algorithms that assess and manage the risk of catastrophic failure in Smart Grids ; (iii) initiate a new type of emergent control resulting from the coordinated interaction of multi-agent distributed networks; (iv) design economic and regulatory mechanisms so that financial incentives to adopt Smart Grids align with the social benefits and costs of microgrid and virtual power plant deployments; and (v) extend the Sustainable Assessment Framework for Smart Grid deployment previously developed to understand and make the profound tradeoffs that society must weigh among economic, social, environmental, technical, reliability, and resiliency objectives. Keywords: smart grids, resiliency, sustainability, emergent control, societal choices, retail markets..

NSF CPS: Frontiers: Collaborative: Addressing Trade-offs Between Robustness, Resiliency and Sustainability in Smart Grids.?
Descrição: Electric power systems are undergoing radical changes characterized by an evolution toward more flexible infrastructures, termed Smart Grids, and triggered by the deployment of new technologies in power electronics, sensing, protection, control, and communications. While these technologies are opening new avenues for enhanced system reliability, resiliency, and sustainability and for enabling proactive involvements of customers in the management of the system, they also introduce new vulnerabilities that, if not properly identified and mitigated, may offset their potential benefits. We propose to address these challenges in two areas, namely, the Science and Engineering of cyber-physical systems. Thus, we will use a systems engineering approach to achieve the following: (i) lay down a theory of resiliency for the massive cascading failure of complex systems; (ii) develop methodologies and algorithms that assess and manage the risk of catastrophic failure in Smart Grids ; (iii) initiate a new type of emergent control resulting from the coordinated interaction of multi-agent distributed networks; (iv) design economic and regulatory mechanisms so that financial incentives to adopt Smart Grids align with the social benefits and costs of microgrid and virtual power plant deployments; and (v) extend the Sustainable Assessment Framework for Smart Grid deployment previously developed to understand and make the profound tradeoffs that society must weigh among economic, social, environmental, technical, reliability, and resiliency objectives. Keywords: smart grids, resiliency, sustainability, emergent control, societal choices, retail markets..

Descrição: Title: "Development of Complex Systems Theories and Methods for Resilient and Sustainable Electric Power and Communications Infrastructures". Project Summary - This project will advance research frontiers by building bridges between the emerging sciences of complex systems and a variety of engineering fields dealing with the design and control of large-scale nonlinear networked systems. Specifically, the proposed research will extend the scope and applicability of the highly optimized tolerance approach to modeling cascading failures across interdependent electric power and communications infrastructures. These models will allow a planner to place resources at strategic locations so that the risk of blackouts is controlled. Because this design approach results in infrastructures that are robust yet vulnerable to unanticipated failures, an agent-based distributed intelligent control scheme will coordinate the dynamics of a host of microgrids. The latter will be supplied by a mix of renewable and fossil-generating units for improved sustainability and resiliency during both equipment failures and natural disasters. Microgrids, operated by businesses interacting in retail markets, will provide customers with appropriate incentives to participate in energy savings and grid survivability during emergency conditions. As for the communications network of a power system, we propose to investigate ?failures containing self-stabilization? techniques that will minimize the recovery time subject to some efficiency loss attributable to the protocol overhead. To cope with unanticipated failures affecting this network, we will consider model-driven software-generation techniques that guarantee freedom from such failures.
